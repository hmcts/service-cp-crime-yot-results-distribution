# Defect-Fix Register

This service is deliberately **not** a bug-for-bug port, and since increment 002 it has **two
oracles**: the JavaScript function app for the intake half, and progression's court-register leg
for the downstream half (constitution v3.0.0 Principle I). The register carries **36 `C` rows** -
the thirty-four defects catalogued in the court-register design (§7, C1-C34; the design is the
Confluence page
[Court Register Service](https://tools.hmcts.net/confluence/spaces/CRA/pages/2004104319/Court+Register+Service))
plus the two appended under review (C35, C36 - see the final rule below) - and **10 `P` rows**: the
nine progression-leg defects the same design catalogues at §3.4 and dispositions at §7.3, which
increment 002 appends here as that leg moves into this service, plus the one appended under review
(P10). Thirty-one of the catalogued `C` defects live in this service and are **fixed**, as are both
appended ones; the other three (C18, C28, C34) are externally-owned remediations registered as
PENDING with an owner and a trigger and tracked to conclusion before cutover — C18 split on
2026-09-14 into C18a (the legacy triggers' stand-down) and C18b (the producer's flag-gated
publisher), two repositories and two owners, so the count of externally-owned rows is four. Of the `P` rows,
seven are FIXED (P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P9, P10), two are RETIRED with the leg (P6, P7) and one is
MOOT (P8). Every fix is recorded here - legacy behaviour, fixed behaviour, the test that pins the
fix, and its sign-off state. The rules:

- A fix merged without a register entry is itself the defect: revert it or register it.
- The differential audit fails the build in both directions — a catalogued defect still reproduced,
  **or** a behaviour change that is not on this register. Legacy remains the oracle for everything
  not catalogued. **As run (T075, 2026-09-01):** 381 recorded legacy runs through the real chain, in
  `./gradlew build`, with no container and no tag; twenty-one rows explain differences and nothing
  is unattributed. The *reverse* reconciliation — that every content-changing row is actually
  reached by the corpus — is a report rather than an assertion, and where a row explained nothing
  the corpus was extended rather than the claim weakened: 351 cases became 381, and every one of
  the earlier recordings is byte-identical after the rebuild. Eighteen of the nineteen
  content-changing rows now appear; the nineteenth is C20, whose trigger the extension proved
  unreachable from any hearing payload.
  Report: `specs/001-court-register-port/checklists/differential-audit.md`.
- Each fix is implemented and pinned by the phase that lands it; a row moves to **FIXED** only in
  the commit whose pinning test passes. Rows marked **sign-off pending** gate **cutover**, not
  implementation: a register produced for a live court centre must not carry an unapproved content
  change.
- Legacy citations are into `cpp-context-azure-legalaidagency/azure-functions/durable-functions`
  (`$DF`) at HEAD `0d63f3ae`.
- **C1–C34 are the design document's catalogue; anything numbered above it was found later** — in
  review or in the differential audit — and appended here rather than shipped as a silent
  divergence. That is the mechanism Principle I requires of an uncatalogued behaviour change:
  it earns a C-number with review, or the legacy behaviour stands.
